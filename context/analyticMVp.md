For an MVP, the objective isn't to measure everything—it's to answer the most important business questions:
Are people buying?
Where are sales coming from?
Which products are performing?
Where are customers dropping off?
Are we making money?
With that in mind, I'd prioritize the following dashboard.

1. Executive KPIs (Top Row)
   These should be visible immediately when opening the dashboard.
   Metric
   Visualization
   Total Revenue
   KPI Card
   Total Orders
   KPI Card
   Average Order Value (AOV)
   KPI Card
   Conversion Rate
   KPI Card
   Total Customers
   KPI Card
   Revenue Growth (%)
   KPI Card

2. Revenue Trend
   Question: How is the business performing over time?
   Chart: Line Chart
   Metrics:
   Revenue by day/week/month
   Orders by day/week/month

3. Sales by Product
   Question: What is selling?
   Chart: Horizontal Bar Chart
   Metrics:
   Top-selling products
   Revenue per product
   Quantity sold

4. Sales by Category
   Question: Which categories generate the most revenue?
   Chart: Horizontal Bar Chart

5. Traffic Source Breakdown (if tracking traffic)
   Question: Where are customers coming from?
   Chart: Donut Chart
   Example:
   Organic
   Google Ads
   Facebook
   Instagram
   Direct
   Referral
   If you're not yet collecting marketing attribution, you can postpone this until later.

6. Conversion Funnel
   This is one of the most valuable analytics for an MVP.
   Chart: Funnel
   Stages:
   Visitors
   ↓
   Product Views
   ↓
   Add to Cart
   ↓
   Checkout
   ↓
   Purchase
   This quickly highlights where users abandon the buying process.

7. Order Status
   Question: What is happening operationally?
   Chart: Donut Chart
   Statuses:
   Pending
   Processing
   Shipped
   Delivered
   Cancelled
   Refunded

8. Customer Growth
   Question: Are we acquiring customers?
   Chart: Line Chart
   Metrics:
   New customers over time
   Returning customers (if available)

9. Inventory Overview
   Only if inventory management is part of the MVP.
   Chart: Horizontal Bar
   Metrics:
   Low stock products
   Out-of-stock products

10. Recent Orders
    Instead of a chart, include a simple table with:
    Order ID
    Customer
    Date
    Total
    Status
    This is useful for day-to-day operations.

What to leave out initially
Many metrics are valuable, but they can wait until you have more data or more advanced needs:
Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)
Cohort retention
Churn analysis
Heatmaps
Advanced customer segmentation
Geographic maps
Ad campaign ROI
Warehouse efficiency
Supplier analytics
Profit waterfall charts
Demand forecasting
Predictive analytics
